\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{Epoch duration selection.}
Experiment~1 selected a 30-second epoch duration for the Proposed-Med configuration using
macro-F1 on the combined dataset as the primary metric. Importantly, Experiment~3 showed
that macro-F1 was largely insensitive to the epoch grid over 20--120\,s, with only small
differences between durations and no evidence of a strong performance cliff. This supports
the practical view that epoch duration is a secondary design choice for the detector
itself; we nevertheless fix 30\,s downstream for consistency and to match the empirically
best setting under the chosen metric.

\paragraph{Strategy comparison.}
Across the five compared conditions, Proposed-Med substantially improved over the two
practical baselines (BLINKER-concat and MNE-annot), but did not dominate the DBO ablation in
macro-F1 under the selected 30-second epoch duration. This suggests that the Stage~A/B
screening-and-estimation design is effective for avoiding the most brittle thresholding
behaviour seen in naive concatenation, yet the direct optimisation approach can still be
competitive or superior in some regimes. A likely explanation is that Stage~A may remove
epochs that contain atypical but valid blink morphologies, trading recall for robustness.
Future work should quantify when Stage~A is beneficial (e.g., heavy artefact sessions) and
when it is overly conservative.

\paragraph{Sensitivity to the evaluation strictness.}
Experiment~4 demonstrated that reported F1 varies strongly with the IoU threshold used for
event matching. This is expected: stricter IoU thresholds penalise boundary mismatch
aggressively and therefore lower F1 even when detections are qualitatively correct.
The main experiments therefore adopt IoU\,=\,0.1 as a pragmatic default, while reporting
the full IoU sweep to make this methodological dependence explicit.

\paragraph{Morphological analysis.}
Experiment~6 (detailed) complements the scalar metrics by visualising typical true
positives, false positives, and false negatives across duration and amplitude strata. This
helps distinguish failures due to boundary misalignment from failures due to missed events,
and provides qualitative evidence about which blink types are systematically challenging.
