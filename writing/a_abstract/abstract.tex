\begin{abstract}
In fatigue-driving studies, blink activity within the electroencephalography (EEG) is treated as arker of driver fatigue rather than noise. Thresholding is one of the most straightforward and widely used approaches for detecting blink activity from EEG signals. However, current approaches that use thresholding estimate a single amplitude threshold from a pool of blink-heavy and blink-free epochs concatenated together, which dilutes the amplitude statistics used for threshold estimation. This study presents an epoch-aware, three-stage pipeline that avoids this dilution by keeping blink-heavy and blink-free epochs separate when estimating the detection threshold. The three-stage pipeline first screened non-overlapping epochs by peak-to-peak amplitude, then estimated a sample-level threshold from the suspicious-epoch pool, and finally extracted blink regions and mapped them to epoch-local coordinates. The proposed pipeline and two established continuous-signal baselines were compared on the complete 32-channel montage across two naturalistic driving-EEG corpora, Internal and Cao2018, using event-level overlap matching and macro-averaged $F_1$. Evaluated on this complete-montage configuration, which produced the highest performance recorded across all analyses, the proposed pipeline reached macro-$F_1$ scores of 88.25\% on Internal and 80.68\% on Cao2018, significantly exceeding both baselines on both corpora after Bonferroni-corrected Wilcoxon signed-rank tests. These results indicate that epoch-aware threshold estimation yields more reliable blink detection than continuous-signal thresholding in naturalistic EEG driving recordings.
\end{abstract}
