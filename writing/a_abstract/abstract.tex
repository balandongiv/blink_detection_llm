\begin{abstract}
    In fatigue-driving research, blink activity in electroencephalography (EEG) is treated as a marker of driver fatigue rather than as noise. Amplitude thresholding is among the most straightforward and widely used approaches for detecting blinks in EEG.
\rev{However, existing thresholding methods typically estimate a single amplitude threshold from continuous signals in which blink-heavy and blink-free segments are pooled together, which may shift the operating point of the resulting detector.}
    This study  present Candidate-Epoch-Conditioned Thresholding (CECT), a three-step pipeline that screens fixed-length epochs for prominent amplitude excursions across channels, estimates one robust threshold per channel from the selected epochs only, and applies each threshold to its channel across all epochs.
%    The pipeline first identifies candidate epochs through multi-channel peak-to-peak amplitude screening, then derives a robust threshold for each channel from these candidate epochs, and finally applies each threshold to its corresponding channel across all epochs to extract channel-wise blink regions.
\rev{CECT was compared with two established continuous-signal baselines on the full 32-channel montage using two naturalistic driving-EEG corpora, an internally collected dataset and a public dataset, with performance evaluated through event-level overlap matching and macro-averaged $F_1$.
CECT achieved macro-$F_1$ scores of 88.25\% on the internal dataset and 80.68\% on the public dataset, significantly outperforming both baselines on both corpora. CECT performance also remained stable across epoch durations from 10 to 120 s. These results indicate that restricting threshold estimation to blink-bearing segments, rather than pooling the entire recording, yields more reliable blink detection in naturalistic driving EEG, and that this benefit does not depend on a particular choice of epoch length.
}
\end{abstract}
