\begin{abstract}
    In fatigue-driving research, blink activity in electroencephalography (EEG) is treated as a marker of driver fatigue rather than as noise. Amplitude thresholding is among the most straightforward and widely used approaches for detecting blinks in EEG. % REMOVED (issue I08): "diluting" stated as fact
\rev{However, existing thresholding methods typically estimate a single amplitude threshold from continuous signals in which blink-heavy and blink-free segments are pooled together, which may shift the operating point of the resulting detector.} This study presents an epoch-aware, three-stage pipeline that avoids this dilution by estimating thresholds only from epochs likely to contain blink activity. The pipeline first identifies candidate epochs through multi-channel peak-to-peak amplitude screening, then derives a robust threshold for each channel from these candidate epochs, and finally applies each threshold to its corresponding channel across all epochs to extract channel-wise blink regions. % REMOVED (issue I19): two naturalistic driving-EEG corpora
% REMOVED (issue I02): headline scores without oracle qualifier
% REMOVED (issue I01): "yields more reliable blink detection than continuous-signal thresholding"
\rev{The proposed pipeline was compared with two established continuous-signal baselines, including a BLINKER-style initial-threshold baseline (threshold-crossing stage only) and the MNE amplitude-annotation routine, on the full 32-channel montage using two simulated-driving EEG corpora, an internally collected dataset and a public dataset, with performance evaluated through event-level overlap matching and macro-averaged $F_1$. Under a retrospective best-channel-per-session (oracle) protocol, the proposed pipeline achieved macro-$F_1$ scores of 88.25\% on the internal dataset and 80.68\% on the public dataset, significantly outperforming both baselines on both corpora. These results indicate that the complete proposed configuration achieved higher event-level $F_1$ than the examined baselines under the evaluated offline protocol; a matched ablation would be needed to attribute the advantage specifically to epoch screening.}
\end{abstract}
