\begin{abstract}
This study evaluated an epoch-aware pipeline for EEG blink-region detection that avoided estimating detection thresholds from concatenated blink-containing and blink-free epochs. The three-stage pipeline first screened non-overlapping epochs by peak-to-peak amplitude using an autoreject-inspired cross-validation criterion, then estimated a sample-level threshold from the suspicious-epoch pool using either a mean-based or median/MAD-based estimator with a fallback for small pools, and finally extracted blink regions and mapped them to epoch-local coordinates. BLINKER-concat, MNE-annot, Proposed-Mean, and Proposed-Med were compared on the Raja and Cao2018 naturalistic driving-EEG corpora using event-level overlap matching and macro-averaged $F_1$. At 30 s, Proposed-Med achieved a pooled macro-$F_1$ of 0.8403, significantly exceeding BLINKER-concat at 0.7256 and MNE-annot at 0.6086. Proposed-Mean and Proposed-Med were statistically indistinguishable ($p=1$), which indicated that the gain came from epoch screening rather than the choice of Stage-B estimator. The channel-selection ablation further showed that a single frontopolar electrode was sufficient for the strongest single-channel detection on both corpora. Finally, pooled macro-$F_1$ varied by only 0.0056 across epoch durations from 10 s to 120 s, and no tested duration differed from the 30 s reference after Bonferroni correction.
\end{abstract}
