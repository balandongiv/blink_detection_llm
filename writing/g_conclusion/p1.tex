Taken together, the findings indicate that screening epoch-structured EEG before sample-level threshold estimation provides more reliable event-level blink-region detection than deriving a single threshold from the concatenated recording under the evaluated driving conditions.
The proposed median-based configuration outperformed the examined baselines across the two driving corpora, supporting epoch screening as the principal methodological contribution.
The spatial analysis further showed that frontopolar electrodes retained most of the full-montage performance, while the epoch-duration analysis indicated broadly stable pooled performance despite some corpus-specific sensitivity to longer epochs. These findings provide an interpretable basis for sparse, epoch-aware blink detection that can accommodate common segmentation choices without relying on a dense electrode montage.
The evaluation intentionally focused on driving data to assess the method within its target fatigue-driving context, yet this lead to broader generalizability and practical deployment remain to be established.
Future work should therefore test the proposed pipeline across independent datasets, recording systems, and non-driving paradigms. Overall, this study identifies epoch screening as a practical foundation for improving threshold calibration in heterogeneous, epoch-structured EEG.
