The study addressed blink-region detection in epoch-structured EEG, for which concatenated-signal methods pooled blink-containing and blink-free epochs when estimating detection thresholds. The proposed pipeline screened epochs by peak-to-peak amplitude, estimated a sample-level threshold from suspicious epochs using either the mean or median centre with MAD-based scaling, applied the threshold to extract blink regions, and mapped the detections to epoch-local coordinates. At 30 s, the proposed variants improved pooled macro-averaged $F_1$ from 0.7256 for BLINKER-concat and 0.6086 for MNE-annot to 0.8345 for Proposed-Mean and 0.8403 for Proposed-Med. This improvement chiefly reflected higher precision, with pooled values of 0.8209 and 0.8127 compared with 0.6182 and 0.6425 for the baselines, while recall remained 0.8789 and 0.8956. Proposed-Mean and Proposed-Med were statistically indistinguishable on session-level $F_1$ ($p=1$), indicating that the advantage arose from epoch-aware screening rather than from the choice of centre.

The remaining experiments showed that the pipeline retained its performance under reduced channel coverage and changes in epoch duration. A single frontopolar electrode recovered most of the full-montage performance: running the complete pipeline on one electrode alone gave $F_1$ values of 0.8337 for Fp2 against the full-montage reference of 0.8825 in Raja, and 0.7765 for Fp1 against 0.8068 in Cao2018. Pooled macro $F_1$ also remained stable from 10 s to 120 s, with a spread of 0.0039 and values ranging from 0.8367 to 0.8406, although on Raja alone performance declined significantly at 50, 60 and 120 s. The predicted blink count tracked the true count closely for both proposed variants, with Pearson $r=0.918$ and Lin's CCC $=0.917$ for Proposed-Mean and Pearson $r=0.941$ and Lin's CCC $=0.938$ for Proposed-Med. This agreement was sufficient to support blink-rate-style measures, although Proposed-Med retained a positive bias of $+80.2$ events and 95\% limits of $[-513.9, +674.4]$.

The conclusions were limited by evaluation on the Raja and Cao2018 driving corpora, the use of a single event-matching tolerance, and the absence of stratification by blink duration. Future work will therefore assess the pipeline on additional recording settings and matching criteria, examine performance across blink-duration groups, and develop the planned dedicated long-closure module.
