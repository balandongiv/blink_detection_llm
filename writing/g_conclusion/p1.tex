Taken together, the findings indicate that screening epoch-structured EEG before estimating sample-level thresholds provides more reliable event-level blink-region detection than deriving a single threshold from a concatenated recording under the evaluated driving conditions.
The proposed median-based configuration outperformed the examined baselines across both driving corpora, supporting epoch screening as the principal methodological contribution of the pipeline.
The spatial analysis further showed that frontopolar electrodes retained most of the full-montage performance, while the epoch-duration analysis indicated broadly stable pooled performance despite some corpus-specific sensitivity to longer epochs. Collectively, these findings support an interpretable and spatially sparse approach to epoch-aware blink detection that can accommodate common segmentation choices without requiring a dense electrode montage.
The evaluation deliberately focused on driving data to establish the method's performance within its intended fatigue-driving context. Consequently, its generalizability beyond this domain and its suitability for practical deployment remain to be established.
Future work should validate the pipeline across independent driving datasets and recording systems before examining its transfer to other epoch-structured paradigms. Overall, this study positions epoch screening as a practical foundation for improving blink-detection threshold calibration in heterogeneous, epoch-structured EEG.
