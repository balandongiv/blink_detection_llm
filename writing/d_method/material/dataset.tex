

Two EEG corpora acquired during sustained simulated-driving tasks were used,
providing 104 complete recording sessions in total. The corpora differed in
their acquisition montages and data provenance, allowing the detector to be
evaluated under both high-density and conventional electrode configurations.

\paragraph{Raja corpus.}
The Raja corpus comprised 46 complete sessions recorded during a simulated
lane-keeping task. EEG was acquired with a 128-channel EGI HydroCel system,
with CPz as the reference. Reference blink intervals were obtained from human
annotations informed by eye aspect ratio (EAR) and electrooculographic (EOG)
signals. Although the original recordings used a high-density montage, the
present analyses retained a predefined subset of 32 electrodes corresponding
to conventional 10--20 scalp locations. These electrodes are reported by their
10--20-equivalent names; Table~\ref{tab:egi_map} provides the corresponding
native EGI indices.





\paragraph{Cao2018 corpus.}
The public Cao2018 corpus contains 62 sessions from 27 participants who
performed an approximately 90-minute lane-keeping task in an immersive driving
simulator~\citep{cao2019multichannel}. The present study included the 58
sessions marked as complete and excluded the remaining four incomplete
sessions. Because ocular activity had been removed from the released
preprocessed data, the raw recordings were used and paired with the available
blink-interval annotations. All 32 recorded electrophysiological channels (30
scalp electrodes and the A1/A2 mastoid reference channels) were supplied to the
pipeline; the concurrently recorded vehicle-position channel was excluded.

Within each corpus, all detector conditions were evaluated using the same
recordings, channel scope, valid-epoch masks, and reference annotations. This
common data scope enabled paired, session-level comparisons between conditions.
