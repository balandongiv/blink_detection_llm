\paragraph{Computation of Sample-Level Blink-Region Threshold Candidates}


Stage~B estimates a sample-level amplitude threshold for each channel using samples drawn from the candidate epochs $\mathcal{B}$ identified in Stage~A.
% REMOVED (issue I21): The estimation adopts the approach by \citep{kleifges2017blinker}, in which each threshold combines...
\rev{Each threshold combines a measure of central tendency with a robust measure of dispersion and a sensitivity parameter, allowing the threshold to adapt to the amplitude characteristics of each channel while limiting the influence of isolated extreme samples. This robust median/MAD construction differs from the initial-detection threshold of~\citep{kleifges2017blinker}, which uses the arithmetic mean plus 1.5 standard deviations of the full recording signal.}


Let \(\mathcal{S}\) denote the set of epochs used for estimating the
sample-level threshold. In the primary analysis,
\(\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{B}\), so that the estimate is driven by epochs already
identified as likely to contain blink-related activity.


For each channel \(c\), all samples from the selected epochs are pooled into a
one-dimensional empirical sample set
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{Y}_c
= \{x_{i,c,t}: i \in \mathcal{S},\; 1 \leq t \leq T\}.
\end{equation}
%This pooling step marks the methodological transition from epoch-level rejection to sample-level localization: the quantities used in Stage B are no
%longer peak-to-peak ranges \(a_{i,c}\), but the individual signal amplitudes
%\(x_{i,c,t}\) that will later be thresholded to form candidate blink regions.

The sample-level threshold is estimated using a robust location-plus-dispersion form. Let \(\xi_c\) be the channel-specific center of the sample distribution % REMOVED (issue I22): which can be express as below:
\rev{defined as follows:}
\begin{equation}
\xi_c = \operatorname{median}_{y \in \mathcal{Y}_c} y,
\end{equation}

%It should be note that, the arithmetic mean may be substituted when a mean-centered threshold
%is required for comparison.
%
The dispersion is estimated by the median absolute
deviation (MAD),
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{MAD}_c
= \operatorname{median}_{y \in \mathcal{Y}_c}
\left|y-\operatorname{median}_{u \in \mathcal{Y}_c}u\right|,
\end{equation}
and is rescaled to a standard-deviation-compatible quantity by
\begin{equation}
\sigma^{\mathrm{rob}}_c = 1.4826\,\operatorname{MAD}_c .
\end{equation}
% REMOVED (issue I21): and is the same scaling choice originally used in the paper \citep{kleifges2017blinker}.
The constant \(1.4826\) is the usual normal-consistency factor for the MAD\rev{, ensuring that the rescaled MAD equals the standard deviation under Gaussian data}.

For a sensitivity parameter \(k>0\), the resulting sample-level blink-region
threshold candidate for channel \(c\) is
\begin{equation}
\theta_c = \xi_c + k\sigma^{\mathrm{rob}}_c .
\end{equation}
The set
\begin{equation}
\Theta = \{\theta_c: c \in \mathcal{C}\}
\end{equation}
constitutes the channel-wise threshold candidates passed to the blink-region extraction stage.

\rev{In the present study, $k=3.0$ was used for the proposed configurations. This value was selected by a sweep over candidate values on the evaluation corpora (see Section~IV for discussion of this limitation). The BLINKER-style baseline used a different threshold rule with $k=1.5$ applied to the arithmetic mean and standard deviation of the full recording, following the initial detection stage of~\citep{kleifges2017blinker}.}
