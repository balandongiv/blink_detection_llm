\paragraph{Processing pipeline overview.}
\label{sec:pipeline_overview}

A. Processing Pipeline Overview

The proposed method is an epoch-aware, double-thresholding pipeline that transforms a continuous multichannel EEG recording into channel-wise blink-region detections through five sequential operations. First, the EEG signals are downsampled and band-pass filtered before being divided into fixed-length, non-overlapping epochs. The proposed double-thresholding method then operates through three detection stages. Stage A performs multichannel peak-to-peak screening to identify candidate epochs containing prominent amplitude excursions. Stage B pools the samples from these candidate epochs to estimate a robust sample-level threshold independently for each channel. Stage C applies the resulting channel-specific thresholds to all valid epochs to localize blink regions and determine their epoch identifier, channel, onset time, and duration. \paragraph{Preprocessing}
First, the continuous recording is downsampled to a common sampling grid of \(100\)~Hz. Standardising the sampling rate makes the
amplitude thresholds and the minimum-duration gate comparable across recordings acquired on different hardware, and reduces the per-sample computational cost of the downstream stages without discarding any information needed to resolve a blink, whose
spectral content lies well below the new Nyquist frequency.

Second, a zero-phase band-pass filter from \(1.0\)~Hz  to \(20.0\)~Hz
is applied which is removes slow drifts and DC offsets at the low end and high-frequency muscle and line noise at the high end, isolating the low-frequency, high-amplitude deflection that characterises an eye blink.

Third, each filtered recording is split into non-overlapping, fixed-length epochs. Epoching is what makes the thresholds
\emph{adaptive}, rather than deriving a single level for the entire session, the pipeline treats each short epoch as a local statistical context.
