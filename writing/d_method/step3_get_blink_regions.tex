\subsection{Extraction of Blink Regions}
In Stage~C, the channel-wise threshold candidates are scanned across the signal in
the manner of the Kleifges crossing detector. Maximal contiguous runs of supra-threshold
samples define candidate blink regions, brief crossings shorter than a minimum-duration gate
are discarded, and the accepted regions are reported as epoch-relative onset and offset
times.

The final stage applies the sample-level thresholds
\(\Theta=\{\theta_c\}_{c=1}^{C}\) obtained in Stage~B to obtain channel-wise
blink regions. Specifically, blink regions are searched for within each valid
epoch \(i \in \mathcal{E}=\{1,\ldots,N\}\) and each channel
\(c \in \mathcal{C}\), by comparing the samples \(x_{i,c,t}\) of that epoch and
channel against the threshold candidate \(\theta_c\) of the same channel.

Candidate blink samples are defined by a threshold-crossing indicator
\[
h_{i,c}[t] = \mathbf{1}\{x_{i,c,t} > \theta_c\},
\qquad t=1,\ldots,T .
\]
After any required polarity normalization, \(h_{i,c}[t]=1\) indicates that the
sample amplitude exceeds the channel-specific blink-region threshold. Blink
regions are then defined as maximal contiguous runs of supra-threshold samples.
The \(j\)-th candidate interval in epoch \(i\) of channel \(c\) is written as the
half-open index range \([s_{i,c,j},e_{i,c,j})\), where
\[
h_{i,c}[t]=1 \quad \text{for all } t=s_{i,c,j},\ldots,e_{i,c,j}-1,
\]
with either \(s_{i,c,j}=1\) or \(h_{i,c}[s_{i,c,j}-1]=0\), and either
\(e_{i,c,j}=T+1\) or \(h_{i,c}[e_{i,c,j}]=0\). Equivalently, the onset
\(s_{i,c,j}\) is the first sample in a run that crosses above \(\theta_c\), and
the offset \(e_{i,c,j}\) is the first subsequent sample at which the signal has
returned to the sub-threshold state.

The final output of this stage is therefore a channel-indexed collection of blink regions, each represented by an epoch identifier, a channel label, an onset index or time, and an offset or duration.
