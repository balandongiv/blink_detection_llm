Figure~\ref{fig:exp1_full_montage_per_electrode} shows the per-electrode detection performance obtained when all 32 channels were supplied to the pipeline. On both datasets, detection quality was concentrated at the frontopolar pair rather than distributed across the frontal region. On Internal, Fp2 and Fp1 reached $F_1$ values of 86.82\% and 86.32\%, respectively. Performance fell at the neighbouring sites AF4 and AF3 to 80.32\% and 73.76\% and dropped further at F3 and F4 to 45.65\% and 45.23\%. On Cao2018, Fp1 and Fp2 reached $F_1$ values of 79.68\% and 78.45\%, respectively, whereas F3 and F4 were lower at 59.51\% and 57.99\%.

The central electrodes showed a dataset-specific asymmetry. On Internal, C3 reached an $F_1$ of 61.06\% while C4 reached only 1.85\%. On Cao2018, C3 and C4 performed similarly at 31.08\% and 30.18\%, indicating that the pronounced C3--C4 asymmetry was specific to Internal.

The posterior electrodes performed markedly worse than the frontal and central sites, and the parietal and occipital groups differed between datasets. On Internal, the four parietal electrodes CP1, CP2, CP5, and CP6 were uniformly near floor, with $F_1$ values between 1.75\% and 2.19\%. The eight occipital electrodes P7, P3, PO3, O1, PO4, E83, P4, and P8 showed slightly more spread, ranging from 1.77\% at P4 to 3.60\% at PO3. On Cao2018, the two parietal electrodes were more informative but remained well below the frontal and central regions, with CP3 and CP4 reaching 17.84\% and 19.56\%. The Cao2018 occipital group was also more heterogeneous than its Internal counterpart. O1 and O2 were near floor at 3.10\% and 3.33\%, and T5 and T6 were intermediate at 9.26\% and 8.30\%. P3 and P4, the most anterior electrodes assigned to this region, reached 12.80\% and 12.67\%. The regional mean $F_1$ on Cao2018 was therefore driven by P3 and P4 rather than by electrodes over the occipital cortex.
