The regional analysis above tested whether a \emph{group} of channels was sufficient as a self-contained detector; this analysis instead isolates what the remaining montage contributes to a single, fixed electrode. Each electrode was scored twice on the same sessions, once as the sole input to the complete pipeline (\emph{single electrode}), and once as that electrode's output within the full-montage run, where Stage A screens epochs using every electrode (\emph{full montage}) (Figure~\ref{fig:exp1_single_channel_stats}). The single-electrode score requires no channel selection, so it is unaffected by the best-channel-per-session oracle used elsewhere. The paired difference, $\Delta F_1 = F_1^{\text{full montage}} - F_1^{\text{single electrode}}$, therefore isolates the gain contributed by the other 31 electrodes through Stage A's cross-channel epoch screening, with the target electrode held fixed.

At Fp1 and Fp2, this gain was not significant on either dataset (adjusted $p > 0.05$), indicating that these two electrodes already capture nearly all of the detectable blink signal on their own. It was $+2.59$ and $+3.45$ percentage points for Fp1 and Fp2 on Internal, and $+2.03$ and $+2.86$ percentage points on Cao2018. By contrast, the gain was significant at every weaker frontal-adjacent electrode (adjusted $p < 0.05$), showing that these electrodes rely on the rest of the montage. On Internal it reached $+5.16$, $+6.17$, $+5.79$ and $+5.26$ percentage points for AF4, AF3, F3 and F4, respectively, and on Cao2018 it reached $+10.21$ and $+8.96$ percentage points for F3 and F4. For these weaker electrodes, the gain was concentrated in recall rather than precision, consistent with the full montage recovering blink epochs that Stage A misses when screening on a single noisy channel.
