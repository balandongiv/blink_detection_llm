Against these oracle-reported results, the cost of replacing the best-channel-per-session rule with a deployable fixed frontopolar choice was small. Every condition in the article was reported at its best-channel-per-session operating point, which constituted an oracle, so fixed-electrode performance was compared with the per-session best electrode of the full montage. For Raja, Fp2 was the better single frontopolar electrode fixed in advance, with $F_1=0.8682$, a mean shortfall of 0.0143 and a median shortfall of 0.0039 against the oracle, and performance within 0.02 of the oracle in 38 of 46 sessions; taking the better of Fp1 and Fp2 gave $F_1=0.8793$, with a mean shortfall of 0.0032, a median shortfall of 0.0000, and performance within 0.02 of the oracle in 43 of 46 sessions. For Cao2018, Fp1 was the better single frontopolar electrode fixed in advance, with $F_1=0.7968$, a mean shortfall of 0.0100 and a median shortfall of 0.0000 against the oracle, and performance within 0.02 of the oracle in 49 of 58 sessions; taking the better of Fp1 and Fp2 gave $F_1=0.8010$, with a mean shortfall of 0.0058, a median shortfall of 0.0000, and performance within 0.02 of the oracle in 54 of 58 sessions. Thus, committing to one frontopolar electrode in advance cost only a small amount of $F_1$ relative to the per-session oracle.
