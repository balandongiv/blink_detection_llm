Against these oracle-reported results, the cost of replacing the best-channel-per-session rule with a deployable fixed frontopolar choice was small.
Every condition in the article was reported at its best-channel-per-session operating point, which constituted an oracle, so fixed-electrode performance was compared with the per-session best electrode of the full montage.
For Internal, Fp2 was the better single frontopolar electrode fixed in advance, with $F_1=86.82\%$, a mean shortfall of 1.00 percentage points and a median shortfall of 0.00 against the oracle, and performance within 2.00 percentage points of the oracle in 38 of 46 sessions; taking the better of Fp1 and Fp2 gave $F_1=88.00\%$, with a mean shortfall of 0.00, a median shortfall of 0.00, and performance within 2.00 percentage points of the oracle in 43 of 46 sessions. For Cao2018, Fp1 was the better single frontopolar electrode fixed in advance, with $F_1=79.68\%$, a mean shortfall of 1.00 percentage points and a median shortfall of 0.00 against the oracle, and performance within 2.00 percentage points of the oracle in 49 of 58 sessions; taking the better of Fp1 and Fp2 gave $F_1=80.00\%$, with a mean shortfall of 1.00 percentage points, a median shortfall of 0.00, and performance within 2.00 percentage points of the oracle in 54 of 58 sessions. Thus, committing to one frontopolar electrode in advance cost only a small amount of $F_1$ relative to the per-session oracle.
