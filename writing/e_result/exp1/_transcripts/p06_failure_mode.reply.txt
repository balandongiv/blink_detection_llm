In contrast to the frontal subsets, the non-frontal subsets failed through different precision--recall patterns in Raja and Cao2018, and their Stage-B thresholds did not follow a common ordering across the two corpora (Table~\ref{tab:exp1_subset_summary}). Each subset re-ran Stages A, B, and C on that subset alone, so it constituted a self-contained Proposed-Med detector and tested whether the subset was sufficient rather than the necessity of a region to the full system. On Cao2018, the parietal, occipital, and posterior subsets retained precisions of 0.4944, 0.4361, and 0.5048, respectively, while recall fell to 0.1168, 0.0770, and 0.1194, with corresponding $F_1$ values of 0.1668, 0.1197, and 0.1736. On Raja, both measures fell for the same subsets: precision was 0.1952, 0.1638, and 0.1810, while recall was 0.0147, 0.0262, and 0.0275, with $F_1$ values of 0.0264, 0.0406, and 0.0426. The sample-level Stage-B blink-region threshold also differed markedly between subsets: on Cao2018, it decreased from 66.6 and 69.3 for the fp1-only and fp2-only subsets through 54.4 for the frontal subset, 33.6 for the central subset, and 29.9 for the parietal subset to 24.0 for the occipital subset. Raja did not show this ordering: the central-right subset had the highest threshold of any subset at 87.8 yet reached one of the lowest $F_1$ values, while the occipital threshold of 40.4 remained close to the full-montage value of 43.2.
