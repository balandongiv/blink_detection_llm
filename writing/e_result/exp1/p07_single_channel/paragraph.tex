Following the channel-subset results above, Experiment 1 further tested whether a single electrode was sufficient as a self-contained detector by running the complete Stage A--Stage C Proposed-Med pipeline separately on each single-electrode selection in both Internal and Cao2018, using 30-second epochs and the median centre. Internal comprised 46 sessions and 6 single-electrode selections (Fp1, Fp2, AF3, AF4, F3 and F4), whereas Cao2018 comprised 58 sessions and 4 single-electrode selections (Fp1, Fp2, F3 and F4). Because each selection contained exactly one channel, these values required no best-channel-per-session oracle, unlike every other condition in the article, and so corresponded directly to what a deployed single-electrode system would produce.

Consistent with the channel-subset results, the complete Proposed-Med pipeline retained most of the full-montage performance when operated on a single frontopolar electrode, whereas performance dropped sharply for single electrodes outside the frontopolar pair. The best single-electrode $F_1$ reached 83.73\% for Fp1 on Internal (vs.\ 86.32\% for the same electrode inside the full montage) and 77.65\% for Fp1 on Cao2018 (vs.\ 79.68\%), a gap of only 2--3 percentage points. Outside the frontopolar pair, the gap to the full montage widened: on Internal, single-electrode $F_1$ was 75.16\% for AF4 (vs.\ 80.32\%), 67.59\% for AF3 (vs.\ 73.76\%), 39.85\% for F3 (vs.\ 45.65\%), and 39.98\% for F4 (vs.\ 45.23\%), while on Cao2018 it was 49.30\% for F3 (vs.\ 59.51\%) and 49.03\% for F4 (vs.\ 57.99\%) (Figure~\ref{fig:exp1_single_channel}).

The size of this gap tracked distance from the frontal pole rather than being uniform across electrodes: on Internal, the shortfall from the full montage widened from 2.59 percentage points at Fp1 and 3.45 at Fp2 to 5.16 at AF4, 6.17 at AF3, 5.79 at F3 and 5.26 at F4; on Cao2018, it widened from 2.03 points at Fp1 to 10.21 at F3 and 8.96 at F4. Because these single-electrode values carry no channel-selection oracle, they indicate that a deployed single-electrode system built on Fp1 or Fp2 would lose only a few points of $F_1$ relative to the full montage, whereas one built on the weaker frontal-adjacent electrodes would give up a much larger share of the achievable performance.
