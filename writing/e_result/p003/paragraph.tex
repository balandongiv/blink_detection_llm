The effect of epoch duration was evaluated at 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, and 120~s, as summarised in Table~\ref{tab:epoch_duration} and Figure~\ref{fig:f1_by_epoch}. Proposed-Med pooled macro-$F_1$ varied only marginally across this range, from 0.8324 at 120~s to 0.8432 at 60~s, a total spread of about 0.011. Two-tailed Wilcoxon tests against the 30~s reference, Bonferroni-corrected over the six non-reference durations, were non-significant for every duration (all corrected $p>0.4$). Performance was therefore statistically indistinguishable across 10--120~s. The 30~s setting was retained as the primary configuration for consistency with common practice in driving-fatigue EEG analysis and for its favourable computational cost, rather than because it maximised $F_1$.
