Accordingly, we hypothesise that naive concatenation will produce systematically lower session-level $F_1$ scores than the epoch-aware pipeline because of a less favourable precision-recall trade-off. To evaluate this hypothesis, all three pipelines (i.e., BLINKER, MNE, and Proposed-approach) are applied to every session using 30-second epochs and all available channels. All 32 channels are included to ensure a fair comparison with BLINKER, which, by default, processes all channels provided to it and requires the user to select a representative blink channel. For consistency across methods, all pipelines are therefore supplied with the same 32 channels, and the channel yielding the highest $F_1$ score is selected for subsequent analysis.
