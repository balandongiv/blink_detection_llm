Taken together, the epoch-duration sweep indicated that Proposed-Med tolerated changes in epoch duration when performance was pooled across Raja and Cao2018, with no tested duration differing significantly from the 30-second reference and a pooled $F_1$ spread of 0.0039 across the seven durations (Table~\ref{tab:epoch_duration}; Figure~\ref{fig:f1_by_epoch}). This tolerance was important because epoch duration in driving studies was determined by the experimental paradigm rather than by the blink detector, so the thresholding procedure needed to accommodate different segmentation choices. The exception was corpus-specific: on Raja, $F_1$ declined from 0.8825 at the 30-second reference to 0.8793 at 50 seconds ($p<0.001$), 0.8773 at 60 seconds ($p=0.002$), and 0.8688 at 120 seconds ($p<0.001$), with the largest decline occurring at 120 seconds. One plausible interpretation was that a longer epoch was more likely to contain both blink and blink-free signal, making its summary amplitude less representative of either state and thereby diluting the screening stage; this would correspond to the same form of dilution that the pipeline was designed to avoid by estimating the threshold from screened epochs, although the present data did not establish this mechanism. Because Raja also declined significantly at 50 and 60 seconds, the safe operating range indicated by these data was narrower than the full tested range, and the 30-second setting was the setting supported on both corpora.
