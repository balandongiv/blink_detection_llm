We therefore hypothesise that the pooled, continuous-signal strategy produces lower session-level $F_1$ than the epoch-aware pipeline because of a less favourable precision-recall trade-off. To test this hypothesis, BLINKER, MNE, and the proposed pipeline were applied to every session of Internal and Cao2018 using 30-s epochs. All 32 channels were included to ensure a fair comparison with BLINKER, which by default processes every channel provided to it. All three methods received the same epochs, band-pass filtered at 1--20~Hz and resampled to 100~Hz, and each baseline was run on the concatenation of these epochs. BLINKER was represented by its threshold-crossing stage but blink marphological-fitting step and automatic channel selection were not used. MNE was represented by \texttt{mne.preprocessing.find\_eog\_events} with its default threshold of one quarter of the range of the signal. Both baselines were run on each channel independently.
For each method and session, the channel with the highest $F_1$ was then retained. This consideration favours no method in particular, since each is evaluated at its best-performing channel.Session-level $F_1$ was compared using two-tailed Wilcoxon signed-rank tests paired by session, with $p$-values Bonferroni-corrected over the four baseline comparisons.
