Beyond the precision--recall operating points, blink-count fidelity also differed across conditions (Table~\ref{tab:count_agreement}; Figure~\ref{fig:count_agreement}). BLINKER-concat produced a mean predicted-to-true count ratio of 1.939, with Pearson $r=0.868$ and Lin's concordance correlation coefficient of 0.714, indicating that it roughly doubled the true blink count despite retaining a positive association with the true counts. MNE-annot produced a count ratio close to one at 1.036, but its Pearson correlation and concordance were lower at $r=0.629$ and 0.617, respectively. Its apparently accurate pooled total therefore resulted from offsetting over-counting and under-counting errors rather than consistent tracking of the true count. Proposed-Mean achieved a count ratio of 1.124, Pearson $r=0.918$, and concordance of 0.917, whereas Proposed-Med achieved 1.157, $r=0.941$, and 0.938, respectively. For Proposed-Med, Bland--Altman analysis gave a bias of +80.2 events, with SD=303.1 and 95\% limits of agreement from $-513.9$ to $+674.4$, showing that it still over-counted on average.
