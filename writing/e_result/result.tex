\section{Experiments and Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:exp_setup}

All experiments are evaluated on the driving corpora described in Section~\ref{sec:datasets}.
Unless otherwise stated, each continuous session is divided into non-overlapping 30-second epochs, and the evaluation is conducted at the event level using the
overlap-based matching criterion introduced in Section~\ref{sec:eval}.

\paragraph{Conditions.}
Four conditions are compared throughout.

\begin{description}
  \item[BLINKER-concat.]
    The BLINKER algorithm is applied to the full concatenation of all valid epochs,
    treating the session as a single continuous signal and deriving a single threshold
    from the entire recording~\citep{kleifges2017blinker}.
    This condition represents the current practice of running a continuous-signal
    detector on epoch-structured data without any epoch-aware adaptation.

  \item[MNE-annot.]
    The MNE-Python \texttt{annotate\_amplitude} routine, which marks contiguous
    segments whose peak-to-peak amplitude exceeds a configurable threshold,
    used here with its default parameterisation.
    This baseline represents widespread preprocessing practice in the neuroimaging
    community and provides a reference point grounded in community tooling.

  \item[Proposed-Mean.]
    The full three-stage pipeline described in Section~\ref{sec:method}, with the sample-level threshold at Stage~B estimated from the arithmetic mean and standard deviation of samples pooled from suspicious epochs.

  \item[Proposed-Med.]
    The three-stage pipeline with the sample-level threshold at Stage~B estimated from the median and median absolute deviation (MAD). This is the primary proposed configuration.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Aggregation.}
Every condition is summarised at its \emph{best-channel-per-session} operating point: for
each session the channel or channel group with the highest event-level $F_1$ is selected,
and those per-session values are then averaged. The same rule is applied to all four
conditions, so no condition is given a selection advantage the others do not receive.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}

\paragraph{Event-level precision, recall, and F1.}
A detected interval is counted as a true positive if it overlaps at least one
unmatched ground-truth blink region; unmatched detections are false positives,
and unmatched ground-truth regions are false negatives.
Per-session precision, recall, and F1 are computed from these event-level counts.

\paragraph{Macro versus micro F1.}
We adopt \textbf{macro-averaged F1} as the primary reporting metric.
Macro F1 computes precision, recall, and F1 independently for each session and
then reports the unweighted mean of the session-level F1 scores.
This gives every session equal weight regardless of how many blink events it
contains. The goal of epoch-based blink detection in driving studies is to produce
a detector that works reliably session by session, not one that merely accumulates
favourable aggregate counts. Micro F1, which pools all true positives, false
positives, and false negatives across sessions before computing a single ratio,
is dominated by sessions with large blink counts and can mask consistent failure
modes on sessions with atypically few blinks.

\paragraph{Wilcoxon signed-rank test.}
Pairwise comparisons between conditions use the Wilcoxon signed-rank test on
matched pairs of session-level F1 scores.
The test is preferred over a paired $t$-test because session-level F1
distributions are bounded and often skewed.
For comparisons in which the proposed method is hypothesised to outperform a
baseline, a one-tailed test is used; stability analyses use two-tailed tests.
Bonferroni correction is applied for the number of conditions compared within
each experiment, and we report exact $p$-values alongside the matched-pairs
rank-biserial correlation $r$ as an effect-size estimate.

\subsection{Experiment 1: EEG Channel-Selection Ablation}
\label{sec:exp1_channel}

\paragraph{Motivation.}
EEG blink artefacts are largest over frontal scalp sites, but it is unclear how much
performance degrades when only a subset of the available channels is used. This experiment
ablates channel groups and individual electrodes to characterise the
performance-versus-coverage trade-off and to identify a compact, reliable channel set.

\paragraph{Design.}
The full pipeline (30-second epochs, median centre) was applied to every session of both
datasets and evaluated \emph{channel by channel}: each electrode's macro-averaged
precision, recall and $F_1$ were read directly from the per-channel rows of the
Experiment~1 results (the \texttt{selection=all\_channel} rows), averaged over sessions,
with no region-level pooling. For the Raja EGI-128 montage the native electrode labels are
accompanied by their 10--20 equivalents through the channel map of
Table~\ref{tab:egi_map}; the Cao2018 montage is already in 10--20 nomenclature.

\subsection{Experiment 2: Strategy Comparison}
\label{sec:exp2_strategy}

\paragraph{Motivation.}
In driving and other epoch-structured paradigms it is common to run a continuous-signal
blink detector on the raw or minimally preprocessed recording and map detections back onto
the epoch grid. BLINKER-concat embodies this practice: its threshold is derived from a
signal pool that mixes blink-heavy and blink-free epochs. The central claim of the proposed
pipeline is that this mixing dilutes the amplitude statistics used for threshold
estimation, distorting threshold placement and shifting the precision--recall trade-off.
MNE-annot provides a second reference point grounded in community tooling: establishing its
performance under an event-level blink criterion, for which it was not designed,
characterises the gap that specialised methods must close.

\paragraph{Hypothesis.}
Naive concatenation will produce systematically lower session-level $F_1$ than the
epoch-aware pipeline through an unfavourable precision--recall trade-off.

\paragraph{Design.}
All four conditions are applied to every session under 30-second epochs on the
\texttt{all\_channel} gate. Per-session macro $F_1$, precision and recall are summarised in
Table~\ref{tab:exp1_main}.

\subsubsection{Effect of the Threshold Estimator at Stage B}
\label{sec:exp2_estimator}

\paragraph{Motivation.}
Stage~B estimates the sample-level threshold from samples pooled across the suspicious
epoch set $\mathcal{S}$. Because $\mathcal{S}$ is selected precisely because it contains
high-amplitude excursions, the pooled distribution is not symmetric around a clean
baseline: it is contaminated by the very peaks the threshold is meant to capture. The mean
and standard deviation are sensitive to these extreme values; the median and MAD are not.

\paragraph{Hypothesis.}
Proposed-Med will outperform Proposed-Mean on precision---because the MAD-based threshold
is less inflated by within-epoch peaks---while achieving comparable or superior recall.

each session the predicted count is $\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FP}$ and the true count is

\subsection{Experiment 3: Stability Across Epoch Durations}
\label{sec:exp3_epoch}

\paragraph{Motivation.}
Epoch duration is routinely chosen on paradigm-specific or theoretical grounds and is
rarely optimised for the blink detector itself. In driving studies, epoch lengths ranging
from a few seconds to several minutes appear in the literature. A robust pipeline should
produce stable event-level $F_1$ across this range, because the underlying physiology of
blinks does not change with the epoch grid imposed during analysis.

\paragraph{Design.}
Proposed-Med is evaluated under epoch durations of 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60 and 120 seconds.
For each duration the full pipeline is re-run from scratch, since epoch-level
peak-to-peak statistics change with epoch length.
Table~\ref{tab:epoch_duration} reports results and two-tailed Wilcoxon $p$-values
relative to the 30-second reference.
