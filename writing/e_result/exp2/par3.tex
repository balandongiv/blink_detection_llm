Across the seven epoch durations, the proposed algorithm maintained consistent pooled performance and similarly stable performance on Cao2018, although its Internal results declined significantly relative to the 30~s reference at the three longest durations (Table~\ref{tab:epoch_duration}, Figure~\ref{fig:f1_by_epoch}).
Pooled macro $F_1$ varied by only 0.39 percentage points, from 83.67\% at 120~s to 84.06\% at 20~s. None of the six alternative durations differed significantly from the 30~s reference after Bonferroni correction, and the smallest corrected $p$-value was observed at 120~s ($p=0.067$).
Performance on Cao2018 was also stable, with an $F_1$ spread of 0.46 percentage points and no significant difference at any tested duration. Although 120~s produced the highest $F_1$ of 81.12\%, exceeding the reference by 0.44 percentage points, this value did not differ significantly from it ($p=0.659$).
Internal showed a less consistent pattern. The 30~s reference yielded the highest Internal $F_1$, 88.25\%, and relative to it $F_1$ fell to 87.93\% at 50~s ($p<0.001$), 87.73\% at 60~s ($p=0.002$), and 86.88\% at 120~s ($p<0.001$). All three differences remained significant after Bonferroni correction, whereas 10, 20 and 40~s did not differ from the reference.
Significance tracked the consistency of the change rather than its magnitude. The 0.32-percentage-point mean drop at 50~s was significant because it recurred across sessions, 35 of 46 falling below the reference with a median change of $-0.40$ percentage points, whereas the larger 0.54-percentage-point mean drop at 10~s was not ($p=1.000$): there sessions split 22 below against 24 above the reference and the median session improved by 0.18 percentage points, so the mean was carried by a minority of sessions with large declines.
The evidence therefore supports stable pooled and Cao2018 performance over the full 10--120~s range and stable Internal performance from 10 to 40~s, but it does not support equivalence on Internal at 50~s and beyond.
