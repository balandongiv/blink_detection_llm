Epoch-duration analysis indicated that pooled macro-$F_1$ was stable overall, with Raja providing the only duration-specific exception at longer epochs (Table~\ref{tab:epoch_duration}; Figure~\ref{fig:f1_by_epoch}). Across the complete 10--120~s range, pooled macro-$F_1$ varied by 0.0056, from 0.8430 at 120~s to 0.8486 at 60~s, compared with 0.8476 at the 30~s reference. Pooled performance was statistically indistinguishable from the 30~s reference at every tested duration, with adjusted $p$-values ranging from 0.097 to 1.000. Cao2018 similarly showed a spread of 0.0071, with no duration differing significantly from the reference. In contrast, Raja showed a wider spread of 0.0163: macro-$F_1$ fell from 0.8882 at 30~s to 0.8720 at 120~s ($p<0.001$), while the value at 60~s was also lower at 0.8837 ($p=0.041$). Performance was therefore stable across 10--60~s overall. The 30~s duration was retained as the primary configuration for consistency with common practice in driving-fatigue EEG analysis rather than because it maximised macro-$F_1$.
