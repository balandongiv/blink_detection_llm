These findings are subject to several limitations. The benchmark was restricted to two driving datasets, so generalisation to other experimental paradigms or clinical populations was not tested. All analyses were also performed offline on recorded data, so performance under real driving conditions, where movement and electrode contact may differ, was not assessed. Channels were chosen retrospectively with the reference annotations, most directly in Experiment~3, where each method's best channel was selected per session. The reported scores are therefore upper bounds for a system whose channel is fixed in advance. Event detection was reported under one overlap-based matching rule, and sensitivity to alternative matching criteria was not evaluated. Absolute $F_1$ values should therefore not be compared across studies without accounting for their scoring rules~\citep{salles2024softed}. Performance was not reported separately by blink duration, so detection of prolonged eye closures, which is particularly relevant to drowsiness assessment, remains unestablished. Finally, the evaluation depends on the available reference annotations and their definitions of blink onset and offset, which can vary with signal representation and annotation criteria~\citep{nystrm2024what}.
