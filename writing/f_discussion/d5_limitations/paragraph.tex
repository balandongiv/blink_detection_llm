These findings are subject to several limitations. The benchmark was restricted to two simulated-driving datasets, so generalisation to other experimental paradigms or clinical populations was not tested. All analyses were also performed offline on recorded data, so performance under real driving conditions, where movement and electrode contact may differ, was not assessed.
Performance was not reported separately by blink duration, so detection of prolonged eye closures, which is particularly relevant to drowsiness assessment, remains unestablished. The evaluation depends on manually annotated reference labels and on their definitions of blink onset and offset, which can vary with signal representation and annotation criteria~\citep{nystrm2024what}. The Internal labels were anchored to synchronised video and the eye-aspect-ratio signal, whereas the Cao2018 labels relied on EEG morphology alone, this difference in annotation modality limits the comparability of absolute scores across the two corpora. In addition, the second rater reviewed the first rater's annotations rather than annotating independently from scratch, so inter-rater agreement was not quantified.
