Experiment~3 showed that the epoch-aware pipeline outperformed both continuous-signal baselines on both driving corpora. Proposed-approach reached macro-$F_1$ scores of 88.25\% on Internal and 80.68\% on Cao2018, compared with 76.54\% and 69.40\% for BLINKER, an established blink-specific method applied to the concatenated recording~\citep{kleifges2017blinker}, and 66.64\% and 56.27\% for MNE, the default amplitude-annotation routine of MNE-Python, with all four session-level differences remaining significant after Bonferroni correction (Table~\ref{tab:f1_significance}). The precision-recall operating points indicate how this gap arose where BLINKER consistently favoured recall over precision, operating at a more permissive point that admitted more false detections, MNE fell lower on both axes, and Proposed-approach occupied the high-precision, high-recall region in each corpus (Figure~\ref{fig:exp2_pr_scatter}). This profile is consistent with the premise that a threshold estimated from a pool of blink-heavy and blink-free epochs is placed too low, and such sensitivity to the signal pool is plausible because ocular-artifact amplitude varies across subjects, electrodes, and recording sessions~\citep{croft2000removal,guttmann2019new,klein2013reliable,kleifges2017blinker}.
