Future work should address these limitations and the open questions raised above. Reporting normal blinks and prolonged closures separately would establish whether performance is retained for the sustained closures most relevant to drowsiness assessment~\citep{tian2021fatigue,shahbakhti2023fusion}. Channel selection should be tested prospectively, with the frontopolar channel fixed in advance rather than chosen from annotated data. A reduced-channel system could then combine a frontopolar channel for blink detection with an occipital channel for spectral features such as alpha activity. Whether a single frontopolar channel can provide both markers should also be examined, since it may carry theta and beta activity~\citep{ko2020eyeblink,shahbakhti2022simultaneous,aoki2023drowsiness} but has weaker alpha and strong ocular contamination. Such systems, and the pipeline itself, should be evaluated online under real driving conditions. Evaluation beyond driving data, including paradigms organised into task blocks, trials, or other natural segments, would test whether the observed robustness extends to different blink morphologies, cognitive demands, and recording conditions.
