The precision--recall profile is consistent with, but does not prove, the proposed explanation that heterogeneous epoch composition affects a threshold derived from the concatenated recording. BLINKER-concat had the highest pooled recall of 96.27\% but substantially lower precision of 61.82\%, whereas Proposed-Med combined recall of 89.56\% with precision of 81.27\%. BLINKER-concat therefore operated at a more permissive point, producing greater sensitivity together with more false detections. Variation in ocular-artifact amplitude across subjects, electrodes, and recording sessions makes sensitivity to the signal pool plausible~\citep{croft2000removal,guttmann2019new,klein2013reliable,kleifges2017blinker}. However, the reported experiments did not directly measure epoch composition, the resulting threshold shift, or the isolated effect of enabling and disabling epoch screening. The supported interpretation is consequently that the proposed pipeline achieved a more balanced operating point; a causal attribution to reduced threshold dilution requires a dedicated ablation.
