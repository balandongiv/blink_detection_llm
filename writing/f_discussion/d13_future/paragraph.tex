Future work should therefore evaluate prolonged eye closures separately, test prospective channel selection, and broaden the benchmark. Reporting normal blinks and prolonged closures independently would establish whether performance is retained for the sustained closures most relevant to drowsiness assessment~\citep{tian2021fatigue,shahbakhti2023fusion}. A prospective study should also replace the reference-based best-channel oracle with a fixed frontopolar electrode or a label-free quality criterion and quantify the resulting performance loss. Evaluation beyond driving data, including paradigms organised into task blocks, trials, or other natural segments, would test whether the observed robustness extends to different blink morphologies, cognitive demands, and recording conditions. Finally, the epoch-screening idea could be assessed as a front end to learned blink or drowsiness detectors~\citep{cabot2024drowsiness,makhmudov2024real,zhang2025fatigue}; such a hybrid remains a proposal and was not evaluated in the present study.
