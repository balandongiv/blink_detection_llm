
The pipeline's advantage therefore arises from screening epochs before threshold estimation~\citep{tran2021amplitude} rather than from whether the retained amplitudes are centred by the median or the mean. The median/MAD centre was selected to reduce the influence of within-epoch peaks that remained in the suspicious-epoch pool, consistent with the general robustness of median-based estimators to outlying observations~\citep{leys2013detecting,rousseeuw1993alternatives}. This rationale was not supported by a measurable performance gain in the present benchmark. Relative to Proposed-Mean, Proposed-Med reduced pooled precision by 0.0081 while increasing recall by 0.0167, yielding only a 0.0057 increase in $F_1$. The two estimators were statistically indistinguishable after correction ($p=1$), and Proposed-Med exceeded Proposed-Mean in 46/104 sessions, with 4 equal and 54 lower. Thus, the median centre did not provide the hypothesised precision benefit and won only about half of the sessions. Its resistance to contaminated amplitude samples makes it a defensible default on statistical grounds, but the present results do not support it as a source of measurable improvement.
