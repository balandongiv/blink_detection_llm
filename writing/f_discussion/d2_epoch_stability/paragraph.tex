Experiment~2 indicated that Proposed-approach, evaluated separately at Fp1 and at Fp2, the two electrodes identified in Experiment~1 as consistently the best-performing, was largely insensitive to epoch duration on Cao2018, where $F_1$ spread by only 0.38 percentage points at Fp1 and 0.33 percentage points at Fp2 across the seven tested durations, with no significant difference from the 30-second reference at any duration (Figure~\ref{fig:f1_by_epoch}). This pattern is useful because epoch duration is commonly determined by the experimental paradigm rather than by the blink detector. Internal showed a less consistent, electrode-dependent pattern. Fp1's $F_1$ declined from 86.32\% at 30 seconds to 85.92\% at 50 seconds ($p=0.002$), to 85.67\% at 60 seconds ($p<0.001$), and to 85.04\% at 120 seconds ($p<0.001$), all three remaining significant after correction, whereas Fp2 declined from 86.82\% to 85.57\% only at 120 seconds ($p<0.001$), with its 50-second and 60-second drops falling short of significance ($p=0.080$ and $p=0.064$). Neither electrode differed from its 30-second reference at 10, 20, or 40 seconds. The evidence therefore supports stable performance from 10 to 40 seconds in both corpora for either electrode, and full stability across the complete 10--120-second range on Cao2018, but it does not establish equivalence across that full range on Internal, where the point at which performance becomes unstable is itself electrode-dependent. Longer epochs may alter the composition of the signal used during screening and threshold estimation, but the present analysis did not test that mechanism directly.
