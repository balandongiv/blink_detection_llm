
The spatial requirements of the pipeline were limited because a single frontopolar electrode recovered essentially all of the full-montage performance in both corpora. Scored within the full montage, Fp2 achieved a macro-$F_1$ of 0.8682 on Raja against the full-montage reference of 0.8825, and Fp1 achieved 0.7968 on Cao2018 against 0.8068; running the complete pipeline on that electrode alone, with no montage behind it, still reached 0.8337 and 0.7765 respectively. The optimum therefore localised consistently to Fp1/Fp2 despite differences in montage and acquisition hardware, while performance declined at electrodes farther from the eyes. This spatial consistency is compatible with the strong projection of blink activity to the prefrontal scalp and indicates that additional channels contributed little to blink-region detection once a frontopolar signal was available. The result also agrees with prior single-channel and forehead-EEG studies that detected blinks or derived fatigue-related measures from prefrontal recordings~\citep{chang2016detection,ko2020eyeblink,zhang2023method,tran2021detection,valderrama2018automatic,wang2025sliding,shahbakhti2022simultaneous,aoki2023drowsiness,lopezahumada2023hardware}. A dense montage is therefore not required for this task, which supports implementations based on a single dry or embedded forehead electrode in wearable and in-vehicle systems where setup time, comfort, and sensor count constrain deployment.
