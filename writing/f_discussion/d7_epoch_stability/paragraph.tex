In contrast to the configuration-dependent cross-dataset differences, epoch-duration sensitivity was limited overall, although the longest tested epoch produced a corpus-dependent decline. Pooled performance was statistically indistinguishable from the 30~s reference at every tested duration from 10 to 120~s, with macro-$F_1$ varying only from 0.8430 to 0.8486. This stability matters because epoch duration in driving studies is usually determined by the experimental paradigm rather than by the blink detector, and the thresholding procedure therefore needs to tolerate different segmentation choices. The exception occurred on Raja, where performance at 120~s fell from 0.8882 at the 30~s reference to 0.8720 and was significantly lower ($p=0.000$). This decline is consistent with dilution of the screening stage: as an epoch becomes longer, it is more likely to contain both blink and blink-free signal, so its summary amplitude becomes less representative of either state. That mechanism is the same whole-recording dilution that the proposed pipeline was designed to avoid by estimating the threshold from screened epochs. The absence of a corresponding pooled decline indicates that the effect was corpus-dependent rather than a general duration sensitivity, but the Raja result argues against treating very long epochs as interchangeable with shorter segments. Epochs of 10--60~s therefore provide the safer operating range.
