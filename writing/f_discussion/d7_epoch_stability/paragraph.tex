Experiment~2 indicated that Proposed-Med was insensitive to epoch duration in the pooled analysis, where macro-$F_1$ spanned only 0.56 percentage points across the seven tested durations and none differed significantly from the 30-second reference after correction (Table~\ref{tab:epoch_duration}; Figure~\ref{fig:f1_by_epoch}). Cao2018 was similarly stable, with an $F_1$ spread of 0.71 percentage points and no significant difference at any duration. This pattern is useful because epoch duration is commonly determined by the experimental paradigm rather than by the blink detector. The corpus-specific results nevertheless qualify the pooled finding: on Internal, $F_1$ declined from 88.82\% at 30 seconds to 88.60\% at 50 seconds ($p<0.001$), 88.37\% at 60 seconds ($p=0.002$), and 87.20\% at 120 seconds ($p<0.001$), and all three differences remained significant after correction. Thus, the evidence supports stable pooled performance and no detected change from 10 to 40 seconds in either corpus, but it does not establish equivalence across the complete 10--120-second range within every dataset. Longer epochs may alter the composition of the signal used during screening and threshold estimation, but the present analysis did not test that mechanism directly.
