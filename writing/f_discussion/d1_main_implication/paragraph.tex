Deriving the amplitude threshold from epoch-screened signal rather than from the whole concatenated recording provides a substantial and statistically significant improvement in blink-region detection across independent driving corpora. Proposed-Med achieved a pooled macro-$F_1$ of 0.8403, compared with 0.7256 for BLINKER-concat and 0.6086 for MNE-annot (Table~\ref{tab:exp1_main}). BLINKER-concat represented an established blink-specific method based on thresholding the concatenated recording \citep{kleifges2017blinker}, whereas MNE-annot represented a community preprocessing baseline related to automated rejection and standardized preprocessing procedures \citep{jas2017autoreject,bigdelyshamlo2015prep}.

The corresponding session-level differences were statistically significant for both BLINKER-concat ($p=1.32\times10^{-11}$) and MNE-annot ($p=6.51\times10^{-9}$), and neither baseline equalled or exceeded Proposed-Med in any channel group on either dataset. These findings indicate that estimating the threshold after excluding unsuitable epochs reduces the influence of recording-wide amplitude heterogeneity and yields more consistent region-level discrimination than applying a single threshold derived from the complete signal.
