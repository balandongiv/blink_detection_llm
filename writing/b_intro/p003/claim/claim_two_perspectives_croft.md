## Exact evidence (literal)

It is thought that the eyelid acts as a sliding potential source, and that this type of ocular activity (blinking) affects the EEG differently than the eye-movements described above [20]. It has been argued that the source of such blinking artifacts is anterior to the source of other types of eye-movement artifact [5].

## Normalized evidence

Eyelid movement during blinking affects the EEG and is explicitly characterized as a blinking artifact.

## Evidence meaning

The paper explicitly adopts an artifact-oriented perspective by describing EEG activity produced by blinking as a "blinking artifact." This supports one side of the two-perspectives distinction: blink activity as contamination. The paper's entire focus on artifact removal represents one of the two major perspectives cited in the manuscript.
