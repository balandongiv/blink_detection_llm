It is important to distinguish between two major perspectives on blink activity in the existing literature~\citep{croft2000removal,tian2021fatigue}. In many EEG studies, blink-related activity is treated as an artifact because it contaminates neural signals and may interfere with the interpretation of brain activity~\citep{croft2000removal,akuthota2023artifacts}. In such cases, blink components are commonly removed during EEG preprocessing~\citep{croft2000removal,klein2013reliable}. However, in fatigue-driving studies, blink activity can also be treated as meaningful information rather than noise~\citep{tian2021fatigue,shahbakhti2023fusion}. From this perspective, blink events are identified, extracted, and analysed as potential indicators of driver fatigue~\citep{ko2020eyeblink,shahbakhti2023fusion}. Blink detection methods can generally be classified into machine-learning-based, morphology-based, and thresholding-based approaches~\citep{agarwal2019blink,zhang2023method}. Machine learning methods rely on trained models to classify blink events, while morphology-based methods identify blinks based on their characteristic waveform shape~\citep{rashida2023quantitative,jiang2023eyeblink}. Thresholding-based methods, in contrast, detect blinks by comparing signal amplitudes with predefined or adaptive threshold values~\citep{chang2016detection,tran2021detection,zhang2023method}.
